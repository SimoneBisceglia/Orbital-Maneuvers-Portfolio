
# ADCS High-Fidelity Simulator: Slew Maneuver and Actuator Trade-Off Analysis

## 1. Mission and Simulation Setup

A high-fidelity attitude dynamics spacecraft simulator was developed to evaluate the attitude-control performance of three actuator architectures: Reaction Wheels (RW), Magnetorquers (MTQ), and Cold Gas Thrusters (THR).

The mission consists of an off-nadir slew in a circular 800-km LEO orbit. The spacecraft transitions from a nadir-pointing attitude to:

* Roll = 30 deg, Pitch = 45 deg, Yaw = 20 deg

with respect to the LVLH frame.

A quintic S-curve time law is applied to the commanded ZYX Euler angles over a 120-s maneuver interval, starting at t = 10 s and ending at t = 130 s. The resulting Euler-angle rates are transformed into body-frame angular velocity and combined with the LVLH orbital rotation to generate the desired ECI-referenced attitude and angular velocity.

The simulation includes aerodynamic drag, solar radiation pressure, gravity-gradient torques, star-tracker and gyroscope errors, and physical actuator limitations.

The simulation horizon extends beyond the 120-s maneuver in order to evaluate post-maneuver settling and steady-state actuator activity.

## 2. Actuator Trade-Off Results

### 2.1 Reaction Wheels

The reaction-wheel architecture provides the smoothest overall dynamic response.

The angular velocity reaches approximately:

* omega_max around 13 x 10^-3 rad/s,

indicating sufficient agility for the considered slew. The response is comparatively smooth, with no significant persistent oscillatory behavior visible in the simulated angular-rate response.

The RW power profile is concentrated mainly during the active maneuver, with a peak of approximately:

* P_max around 0.4 W.

After the main slew, the power decreases substantially.

**Assessment:** RW provides the most favorable combination of agility, continuous torque control, and smooth post-maneuver behavior among the three architectures evaluated. Formal verification of the 120-s requirement should be based on the attitude-error deadband at the end of the maneuver.

### 2.2 Cold Gas Thrusters

The thruster architecture provides angular rates comparable to the reaction-wheel system, reaching approximately:

* omega_max around 12 x 10^-3 rad/s.

Therefore, the thruster system has sufficient dynamic authority to execute a rapid slew and should not be classified as an inherently slow architecture.

The main limitation is the discrete On/Off control behavior. The angular-velocity and attitude responses exhibit persistent oscillations and a clear limit-cycle after the main maneuver, indicating degraded settling and fine-pointing performance.

Under the simulated sensing and control conditions, the high-frequency switching of the thrusters produces continuous actuator activity during the steady-state phase. This behavior can lead to unnecessary propellant expenditure.

**Assessment:** THR provides adequate agility but significantly poorer fine-pointing and settling behavior than RW for the implemented control architecture. It is therefore less suitable as the primary fine-pointing actuator for this maneuver.

### 2.3 Magnetorquers

The magnetorquer architecture exhibits substantially lower angular-rate authority, with a peak of approximately:

* omega_max around 5 x 10^-3 rad/s.

The corresponding attitude response is strongly delayed relative to the commanded maneuver, and the required target attitude is not demonstrated within the 120-s maneuver interval.

The actuator power profile reaches the simulated clipped maximum of approximately:

* P_max around 22.5 W,

indicating that the actuator is operating at or near its modeled power limit over a substantial portion of the maneuver.

Due to the fundamental magnetic torque relationship (cross product between magnetic dipole and local geomagnetic field), the actuator is intrinsically underactuated, since no torque can be generated parallel to the local magnetic field vector.

**Assessment:** MTQs provide insufficient control authority for the considered agile, time-constrained slew. Their performance is therefore inferior to both RW and THR for this specific mission scenario.

## 3. Comparative Assessment

| Parameter | Reaction Wheels | Cold Gas Thrusters | Magnetorquers |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Peak angular rate** | ~13 x 10^-3 rad/s | ~12 x 10^-3 rad/s | ~5 x 10^-3 rad/s |
| **Slew agility** | High | High | Low |
| **Control modulation** | Continuous | On/Off | Magnetic dipole command |
| **Post-maneuver behavior** | Smooth | Limit-cycle / chattering | Delayed |
| **Main limitation** | Momentum accumulation | Propellant consumption / switching | Limited torque authority |
| **Suitability for this slew** | Best | Moderate | Poor |

The results indicate that the three architectures are limited by different physical mechanisms:

* **RW:** best dynamic and settling behavior
* **THR:** adequate agility, poor fine-pointing
* **MTQ:** insufficient authority for the required slew

For the specific 800-km LEO off-nadir maneuver and the implemented PD-control architecture, the Reaction Wheel configuration provides the most favorable overall performance.

## 4. Engineering Interpretation

The results are consistent with the typical operational roles of the three actuator classes.

* **Reaction Wheels** are well suited to precision pointing and agile attitude maneuvers because they provide continuous, finely controllable torque without propellant consumption. Their principal long-duration limitation is momentum accumulation, which generally requires momentum unloading.
* **Magnetorquers** are particularly useful for detumbling, safe-mode control, and momentum dumping. Their dependence on the geomagnetic field and intrinsic underactuation makes them less suitable for fast, unrestricted three-axis slews.
* **Thrusters** provide high instantaneous control authority and can be useful for coarse attitude control, rapid maneuvers, and momentum unloading. However, their discrete actuation and finite propellant budget make them less attractive for continuous fine-pointing in the configuration investigated here.

## 5. Conclusion

The high-fidelity simulations show that actuator selection strongly affects not only maneuver speed but also settling quality and resource usage.

For the investigated mission:

* RW provides the best overall attitude-control behavior, combining high agility with smooth regulation.
* THR provides comparable slew authority but suffers from persistent limit-cycle behavior under the implemented On/Off control strategy.
* MTQ is unable to provide sufficient control authority to demonstrate completion of the required maneuver within the 120-s window.

Therefore, Reaction Wheels are the preferred primary actuator for the considered agile off-nadir maneuver, while MTQs and thrusters remain valuable as complementary actuators for other ADCS functions such as detumbling, momentum unloading, or coarse control.

The final verification of the 120-s requirement should be performed using a quantitative attitude-error metric and a defined settling deadband rather than from the attitude plots alone.
